# v0.5.5

## Highlights

- `pai-sho invite --qr` also draws the invitation as a QR code in the terminal, for the iOS app to scan off the screen. It's drawn for a dark terminal.
- An iPhone, iPad and Mac app, in `apple/`. It runs pai-sho inside the app. On iPhone and iPad it scans an invitation's QR code; on the Mac you paste the invitation. It lists your peers and their ports, and opens a port's page. It isn't on the App Store yet.
- pai-sho also builds as a library, with a C header in `include/pai_sho.h`, which the app links.
- A new `Sleep` request makes a daemon say goodbye to every connected peer and stop dialing until it restarts. The iOS app sends it when it goes to the background. A peer told goodbye stops dialing the sender and waits for it to call, and `list` shows it as `asleep`. An older daemon can't read the goodbye, so it drops it and keeps dialing as before. See docs/adr/0009-goodbye-on-sleep.md.
- A daemon that took up a peer's invitation now dials that peer again after a restart. Before, it forgot to, and waited for a call. This is kept as `dial` in the peers file, which older versions ignore.
- `list` shows `dialing` for a peer being dialed, and `bound_at`, the local address each of its ports listens on.
